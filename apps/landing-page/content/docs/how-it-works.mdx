The runtime is a loop of six stages. Each stage does one job and can be checked on its own. Rules decide; your signature is the last step before money moves. Not every stage runs today, so each one below carries its status from the [status table](/docs#status-of-every-capability).

## Observe

<StatusBadge capability="market-signals" qualifier="hosted" />

Every day, Zap Pilot-hosted jobs collect token and stock prices, crypto Fear & Greed, the CNN US-equity Fear & Greed index, and the positions of connected wallets from DeBank and the Hyperliquid API. From these the strategy reads three signal families: 200-day moving averages, Fear & Greed, and ETH/BTC relative strength.

## Evaluate

<StatusBadge capability="reference-strategy" qualifier="advisory" />

The reference strategy, [DMA/FGI Portfolio Rules](/docs/track-record/dma-fgi-portfolio-rules-v2), runs once a day on Zap Pilot-hosted servers. It walks five rules in priority order; the first rule that fires sets the day's target allocation, and guards limit churn. The output is a decision packet: the target, the rule that fired, and the raw signals behind it. It is advisory. Nothing executes because a rule fired.

## Plan

<StatusBadge capability="rebalance-plans" />

The planner will turn the gap between a strategy target and your current positions into transactions. It does not exist yet.

What runs today is narrower: when you start a deposit, the hosted plan service builds a deposit plan <StatusBadge capability="deposit-plans" /> into positions you hold directly — the Spark USDC vault on Morpho (Base), GMX v2 BTC/USD and ETH/USD GM pools (Arbitrum) and Hyperliquid HLP — routed through LI.FI where funds must be swapped or bridged. The default mix is a fixed starting point you can edit; it does not follow the strategy's target. Withdrawals have a server endpoint but are not offered in the app yet <StatusBadge capability="withdrawals" />.

## Check

<StatusBadge capability="pre-sign-checks" />

Every plan passes a fail-closed gate before it is returned to you:

- **Approvals are capped.** An unlimited approval fails the plan, and an approval of the token you deposit can never exceed the amount you entered.
- **Minimum received.** Every routed swap or bridge must quote a positive minimum received within 1% of its estimate.
- **Simulation.** The source-chain batch of approvals and calls is simulated with Tenderly. A batch that fails simulation is rejected. Production requires the simulator to be configured; if the provider itself times out, the plan is not blocked.

Hyperliquid's HyperCore follow-up steps are not EVM transactions and are not simulated.

A policy of your own — limits, allowlists, a kill switch — checked before anything is signed is planned <StatusBadge capability="policy-engine" />.

## Sign

<StatusBadge capability="wallet-signing" />

The plan is handed to your wallet, where it does nothing until you sign. The app picks the safest path your wallet supports:

<Tabs items={['Atomic batch (EIP-5792 / 7702)', 'Sequential fallback']}>

<Tab>

Wallets that advertise the EIP-5792 atomic capability (`wallet_getCapabilities` returns `atomic.status: "supported"`) get a true atomic batch via viem's `sendCalls`. Every call succeeds together or reverts together, with no half-applied state.

Implementation: `packages/intent-engine/src/execution/eip7702.executor.ts`.

</Tab>

<Tab>

Wallets without atomic capability send the same plan as sequential transactions from your address: the approval first when required, then each prepared call. This preserves the caller identity that LI.FI and ERC-4626 vaults expect.

Implementation: `packages/app-core/src/lib/wallet/executeDepositPlan.ts`.

</Tab>

</Tabs>

Capability detection, and the choice between the two paths, is handled by `packages/intent-engine/src/execution/capability.detector.ts`. Privy embedded wallets also sign an EIP-712 `ZapPilotIntent` message bound to the batch before it is sent.

Hyperliquid deposits are the one place with a standing permission <StatusBadge capability="device-agent-key" />: they use an agent key that is created and stored on your device (browser storage on the web). You approve that key once, and the approval may not expire.

## Verify

<StatusBadge capability="snapshot-chain" />

Every day a snapshot of one reference address is published to IPFS and linked to the previous one by CID. The snapshots are unsigned today. Separately, version 1's exit rule can be recomputed on-chain <StatusBadge capability="verifiable-rule" /> with the [verifiable strategy calculator](/docs/track-record/verifiable-strategy).

## The gate in front of unattended

<StatusBadge capability="unattended-runs" />

Running without you is not the same as having a wallet that can batch. Ordinary EIP-7702 delegation is not a scoped session key. Unattended runs will require on-chain target and method restrictions, native and ERC-20 spend limits, expiry, and revocation you control independently of Zap Pilot's servers — and a prototype and audit review before they are enabled.

## Deterministic first

<StatusBadge capability="ai-exception-layer" />

Today every decision comes from deterministic rules, and the same inputs always produce the same target. An optional AI layer that handles exceptions, bounded by your policy, may come later. It will never be the thing that decides your allocation.

<Callout type="warn" title="What changes vs. what stays">
  Strategy parameters — thresholds, cooldowns and guard settings — live in
  `apps/analytics-engine` and can change in place; the strategy is not versioned
  yet. The signal families (200-day moving averages, Fear & Greed, ETH/BTC) and
  the signing paths described here are stable.
</Callout>
