Zap Pilot is building a self-hosted runtime for programmable portfolios. A strategy you can read produces a target allocation, and the runtime is being built to turn that target into checked transactions you sign from your own wallet.

Today the loop is partial:

- **Strategy.** One reference strategy, DMA/FGI Portfolio Rules, is evaluated daily on Zap Pilot-hosted servers. It produces an advisory target allocation and a decision packet that names the rule that fired.
- **Deposits.** Deposits you start yourself are built as plans, checked, and signed from your wallet into positions you hold directly on Morpho, GMX v2 and Hyperliquid.
- **Not yet.** Turning a strategy target into a rebalance, writing your own strategy or policy, and running the runtime on your own machine are planned. Nothing executes without your signature.

## Principles

- **Strategy is the primitive.** A strategy turns signals into a target allocation and explains each decision. Adapters only encode protocol actions; they never pick strategy.
- **No Zap Pilot vault.** Positions sit at your own address inside third-party protocols. There is no Zap Pilot vault or custody contract, but the protocols themselves are pooled and carry their own risks.
- **Adapters follow exposure.** An adapter is added when a strategy needs an exposure, not to lengthen a list of venues.
- **Durable sources of return.** Positions earn from lending demand, trading fees and market making rather than incentive programs, and every one of them can lose money.
- **Deterministic first.** Rules decide; no model decides an allocation. An optional AI layer for exceptions, bounded by a policy you set, may come later.
