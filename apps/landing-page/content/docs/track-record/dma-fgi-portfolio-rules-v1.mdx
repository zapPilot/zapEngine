## Status

**Strategy ID:** `dma_fgi_portfolio_rules`

<StatusBadge
  capability="reference-strategy"
  qualifier="daily · hosted · advisory"
/>

The strategy is evaluated once a day on Zap Pilot-hosted servers. Its output is an advisory target allocation and a decision packet; nothing executes because a rule fired.

**Not versioned.** The strategy config is global and can change in place. The `v1` in this page's URL is a label, not a pinned version. Versioned strategies are planned <StatusBadge capability="strategy-versioning" />.

## Philosophy

Buy in fear, defend in greed. The rules add risk when trend and sentiment say the market is cheap and scared, and trim it when the market is stretched and euphoric.

## Aim

Improve risk-adjusted results versus [DCA Classic](/docs/track-record/dca-classic-v1). This is a design goal, not a promise.

## Signals

- **200-day moving averages** for SPY, BTC and ETH: which side of its average each asset is on, when it crosses, and how far above it trades.
- **Fear & Greed**: the crypto Fear & Greed index for BTC and ETH, and CNN's US-equity Fear & Greed index for SPY.
- **ETH/BTC**: the ratio against its own 200-day average, for rotation inside the crypto sleeve.

## Rules, in priority order

On each daily snapshot the strategy walks this list top-down. The first rule whose trigger conditions are met produces that day's target allocation; the rest are skipped. A same-day cross-down always wins over a same-day cross-up. Exact thresholds and weights are code-owned in `apps/analytics-engine` (`portfolio_rules/__init__.py`) rather than duplicated here.

### 1. Cross-down exit

When a risk asset closes below its 200-day average, the strategy moves out of that asset — and its peers in the same risk class — into the defensive leg, in full and immediately. This rule can be [recomputed on-chain](/docs/track-record/verifiable-strategy) for a recorded exit.

### 2. Cross-up equal-weight

When one or more risk assets close back above the average, the strategy resets risk to an equal split across the assets above it that day.

### 3. ETH/BTC ratio rotation

When the ETH/BTC ratio crosses its own 200-day average, the crypto sleeve rotates fully to the stronger asset until the next ratio cross.

### 4. ETH/BTC deviation DCA

When ETH/BTC deviates far from its long-term mean, the strategy leans toward the relatively cheap side in small, fixed steps.

### 5. DMA overextension DCA sell

When a risk asset trades far above its 200-day average, the strategy trims a small slice on each eligible day, moving the proceeds into the defensive and equity legs.

### 6. FGI downshift DCA sell

When sentiment moves out of greed into a more neutral or fearful regime, the strategy trims risk in small steps — dialing exposure back before the trend itself breaks.

When no rule fires, the strategy **holds**. That is the common case.

## Guards

- **Cross cooldown.** After an actionable cross, repeat cross actions are suppressed for 30 days (14 for the S&P 500 sleeve), tracked per asset.
- **Rule cooldowns.** After a rule acts it cannot fire again for a fixed spacing: 7 days for the overextension and FGI-downshift sells, 14 days for ETH/BTC deviation steps (60 for the largest tier), and 30 days for cross-up and ETH/BTC rotation.
- **Trade quota.** Minimum spacing between trades and rolling 7-day and 30-day caps are available, but off by default.

## Backtest method

The published backtest is hypothetical. It is recomputed daily over a rolling 500-day window, starting from $10,000, and compared with DCA Classic over the same window.

- **Fills.** Signals are read from a day's closing prices, so a simulated trade is filled the next day at that day's prices. A trade decided on the last day of the window is never filled.
- **Costs.** Every simulated trade pays a fixed 0.3% slippage. There is no separate gas or protocol-fee model.
- **Assumed yield.** Stablecoins earn an assumed fixed 3% APR. BTC, ETH and the S&P 500 sleeve earn nothing. DCA Classic receives the same assumption.
- **Risk-adjusted ratios.** Sharpe and Sortino measure returns above that 3%, not above zero.
- **S&P 500 sleeve.** The backtest holds an S&P 500 sleeve that Zap Pilot cannot execute today.
- **Trades.** Trade counts are simulated trades, not executed ones.

## Status boundary

These rules are evaluated daily and validated by backtests. They are not a claim of executed trades, a live account, automatic rebalancing, or an operating fallback policy. One rule, the cross-down exit, can be [recomputed on-chain](/docs/track-record/verifiable-strategy) as a research check <StatusBadge capability="verifiable-rule" />; the other five cannot.
