# Headline policy

This is the shared title policy for every platform. Platform files add format
limits (length, hashtags, URLs) and never restate or contradict what is here.

## What a title has to do

A post is served to a small first audience. It travels further only if that
audience reacts — saves it, sends it to someone, argues with it. Clearing the
first pool, not being clicked once, is the job.

Judge a candidate title on three axes and keep the one that is strongest
overall, not the one that is safest:

1. **Value** — the reader learns something they did not already know, stated
   plainly enough that they get it from the title alone.
2. **Self-relevance** — a reader outside this industry can tell within one
   reading why this touches them: their money, their job, their prices, their
   country, a company they use.
3. **Social relevance** — the reader can imagine forwarding it. That means a
   stake, a reversal, or a number worth repeating — not a topic announcement.

A title that scores only on (1) is a headline for people already in the
industry. Those cap out with the audience that already follows the space.

## Write for outsiders

The reader is not a crypto or finance native. Trade-press construction —
stacked tickers, protocol names with no gloss, institution acronyms, "TVL",
"L2", "RWA" used as if familiar — reads as noise to everyone else and buys
nothing from the people who do recognize it.

Keep the precise term when it *is* the story, and let the sentence around it
carry the meaning. Prefer the consequence over the mechanism: what changed for
someone, not which instrument moved.

## How to use the publisher headline

The prompt supplies `Publisher headline:` — how the source outlet actually
headlined this story, alongside the episode's own editorial title.

Use it as **evidence**, never as a template.

- It tells you which subject the story is really about, and which facts the
  publisher judged load-bearing. Trust it over your own reading of the
  transcript on the question of what the story is.
- **Keep the named subjects it identifies.** They are what make the story
  specific and searchable.
- **Rewrite the sentence.** Do not reuse its construction, its clause order or
  its emphasis.
- **Never produce it with words swapped.** Replacing each term with a synonym
  and keeping the shape is both derivative and worse — it inherits a headline
  written for that outlet's readers, not for this feed. A title that tracks the
  publisher's wording too closely is rejected and must be rewritten from the
  finding, not edited word by word.

## Which names to keep

The rule turns on the role the name plays, not on how famous it is.

- **Keep the subject.** A company, product, protocol, institution, asset or
  country that the story is *about* stays in the title. Removing it makes the
  story unidentifiable.
- **Drop the source.** An analyst, trader, executive or commentator who is
  merely the person *saying* the thing is not the subject. A reader who does
  not recognize them learns nothing from the name, and it displaces the finding
  itself. Lead with the claim instead.

If both are present, the subject wins the space.

## Be concrete

Every title carries at least one of: a proper noun, or a number that matters.
A title with neither describes a category rather than an event.

Prefer the specific quantity over the direction. "Fell sharply" is a shape;
"fell to 140" is a fact.

## Do not manufacture pull

Curiosity has to come from the finding. Do not invent certainty, urgency, a
return, a prediction, or a stake the episode does not support. Do not
withhold the subject to force a click — a title the reader cannot parse is not
a hook, it is a cost.

Banned as empty: 迎來新發展 / 值得關注 / 深度解析 / 帶來新機遇 / 全面解讀 /
一文看懂 / 你需要知道的 and their equivalents in any language. They occupy the
space a fact should hold.

## Worked examples

Strong — subject named, consequence stated, number a reader can repeat:

- `輝達為何129億買HuggingFace`
- `13億變70億，Stripe買的是什麼？`
- `晶片制裁沒鎖死中國AI，反逼出怪物`
- `庫克告別蘋果：最自豪的不是市值15倍`

Weak — and why:

- `Hayes預測歐元兌日圓跌到140` — leads with a source nobody outside the space
  recognizes. The claim is the story; the name is not. Better: lead with what
  would have to be true for the euro to reach 140.
- `碎片化不等於流動性：房地產代幣化` — two abstractions and no actor. Nothing
  here happened to anyone.
- `宏觀交易者：美聯儲的終極任務是美債` — an unnamed source plus institutional
  vocabulary; the reader cannot tell what changed.
- `AI Roll-up：不賣AI，先買客戶` — an industry term of art used as the hook.
  State the move in words an outsider already has.
